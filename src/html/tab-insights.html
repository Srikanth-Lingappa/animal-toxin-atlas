Two research-oriented cuts of the data: which large well-populated protein families still have no solved 3D structure, and how confidently AlphaFold models them; and which toxins are reported as most potent. (For WHO-priority medically important snake species, see the dedicated Clinical Importance tab.)

How to read this — caveats
Values are parsed from free text that UniProt curators transcribed from the literature, so treat this as a starting point for literature review, not a validated cross-study ranking.

In vivo = LD50/PD50 from the “Toxic dose” field. Mass doses are converted to mg/kg-equivalent. Molar doses (such as nmol/g) are converted with the molecular weight of the stored mature chain and flagged “≈”, but are skipped for heterodimers and other multi-chain toxins, where one stored chain would give the wrong weight. Bounds (“>20 mg/kg”, “below 5 µg/kg”) are not treated as values. Doses in other units (µg/mL on cells, mouse units, per-insect amounts) are not parsed, so roughly 1 in 9 records that mention an LD50 are not listed here.

In vitro = IC50/EC50/Kd/Ki found in the function text, converted to nM. A list such as “IC50 of 369, 386 and 287 nM” is split into separate values, and a range keeps its lower end. Concentrations given as mass per volume (µg/mL) are not converted.

A record with several measurements is ranked by its lowest value — its best route or target, not an average. Routes, species, assay conditions and targets are not standardized across studies, and the target type tag (channel/receptor, enzyme, cell) is a keyword-based guess.

Checked: a 50-record spot check against the UniProt text (25 in vivo, 25 in vitro) found the parsed numbers correct in 48; the two errors (a “below” bound read as a value, and a heterodimer dose converted from one chain) are fixed, along with several wrong target-type tags. It is a sample, not a guarantee.

How to read this
Protein families with at least 12 characterized members. Position shows the share of members with an experimentally solved structure (PDB): families near the bottom, especially large ones at 0%, have no solved structure. Colour and the number on each card show how confident AlphaFold is about the members that are not solved: the mean pLDDT of their predicted models (>90 very high, 70–90 confident, 50–70 low, <50 very low). A family that is both unsolved and confidently modelled is where a prediction can stand in for an experiment today; an unsolved family with low confidence needs the experiment. The mean is shown only when at least 5 unsolved members have a model. pLDDT is computed over the whole translated precursor, including signal and propeptide regions (for 14 records AlphaFold modelled only part of the sequence, and only that part counts).

Each dot is one protein family. Height is the share of its members that are solved; colour is AlphaFold's mean confidence for the members that are not. Click a dot for the full family.
