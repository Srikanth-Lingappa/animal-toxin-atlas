About this atlas

The data
UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot Tox-Prot

This atlas covers 8,274 reviewed UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot records of animal venom proteins, curated by Tox-Prot, UniProt's programme for animal toxins. 86% carry UniProt's “Toxin” keyword; the rest are mostly proteins expressed in the venom gland, such as hyaluronidases and phospholipases. Tox-Prot includes proteins from venomous animals such as snakes, spiders, scorpions, cone snails and centipedes, and from poisonous animals without a venom apparatus, such as some toads and ticks. Each record has the same curation standard as the rest of Swiss-Prot.

The data are a UniProt export from 26 Sep 2026 (Metazoa, taxonomy ID 33208), including InterPro cross-references. Every record links back to its UniProt entry, which remains the authoritative source.

What the atlas adds
views and values built on top of the records

Records can be browsed, filtered, compared and exported, and each tab gives a different view: organisms and their NCBI taxonomy, disulfide connectivity patterns, WHO-listed snakes, embedding maps and shared targets, statistics, structural gaps and potency, and a local Smith-Waterman similarity search.

Some values are worked out here and are not UniProt fields: the animal group (from the NCBI lineage), the toxin class (a grouping of UniProt protein families defined for this atlas; records with no family annotation have no class), disulfide connectivity patterns, potency and lethality (parsed from UniProt free text), computed sequence properties (pI, charge, GRAVY; calculated from the stored sequence, without cleavage or modifications), and the AlphaFold and embedding summaries below.

AlphaFold confidence
AlphaFold DB, model version 6, retrieved 12 Oct 2026

Per-residue pLDDT comes from AlphaFold DB and is shown as a strip on each record's sequence map, in Statistics and in Structural Gaps. The bands follow AlphaFold's own: above 90 very high, 70–90 confident, 50–70 low, below 50 very low. Means cover the sequence AlphaFold modelled, usually the whole precursor, so signal and propeptide regions are included. Records with a solved structure still show the strip, because solved structures usually cover only the mature toxin.

Embedding maps
ProtT5 and ProstT5, projected with UMAP

The two maps in Toxin Relationships use protein language model embeddings of each record's mature region (ProtT5 for sequence, ProstT5 as a structure-aware model), reduced to two dimensions with UMAP (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0.1, random_state 42). They were computed outside this page. Cluster shapes and distances are not quantitative, and the nearest-neighbour and model-agreement views are measured on these 2D coordinates, so they are approximate.

WHO medically important snakes
a second data source

Records whose organism matches a species in the WHO snake antivenom guidelines (Annex 5, TRS No. 1004, Tables A5.3–A5.6) carry a WHO badge, filterable in Browse and broken down by subregion in Clinical Importance. Species are matched by binomial name only. The list is WHO's, not a Tox-Prot annotation.

How to cite
the atlas, its data, and the methods behind it
