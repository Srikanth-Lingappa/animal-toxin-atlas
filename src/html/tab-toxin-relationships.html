How toxins relate to each other beyond their taxonomy: which toxins look alike to a protein language model, and which molecular targets are hit independently by unrelated lineages.

A protein language model's learned embedding for every record in this dataset, reduced to 2D (UMAP). Where UniProt annotates a mature chain or peptide, that region was embedded (signal peptides and propeptides removed; multi-chain toxins as their chains joined); records with no such annotation were embedded in full. Each record’s detail panel states what was embedded. The embeddings were computed outside this page and supplied as coordinates. Fragment records (incomplete sequences) were embedded as deposited, so their positions may be less reliable. No human-assigned labels are involved: proximity reflects what the model considers similar, so toxins from unrelated lineages that land together are candidates for shared function or fold.

Model agreement colors each toxin by how many of its 15 nearest neighbours are the same in both maps; low overlap means ProtT5 (sequence) and ProstT5 (structure-aware) disagree about what it resembles — candidates for fold-versus-sequence questions. Overlap is measured on the 2D UMAP coordinates, not the original embeddings, so some disagreement is projection noise. Tip: color by Disulfide pattern and compare ProtT5 vs ProstT5 — if same-colored points cluster in one but not the other, that's a real signal about fold (ProstT5) versus sequence (ProtT5).
